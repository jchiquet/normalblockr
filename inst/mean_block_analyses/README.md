# Mean-block analyses

Exploratory analyses and simulation studies backing the design choices of the
mean-block family (`normal_block(..., model = "mean")`). Kept out of the built
package (`.Rbuildignore`); the scripts are meant to be sourced from the package
root with `devtools::load_all()` already done. All results below were
re-run on 2026-11-17, after the criteria (#55), `refine()` (#56) and
scale-default (#60) changes.

Not to be confused with `inst/CSDA_analyses/`, which holds the analyses of the
published article on the *variance*-block model.

| script | what it establishes |
|---|---|
| `deviance_monotonicity.R` | Deviance must not increase with `q` (nested models), so every violation measures an optimization failure. On `brca_rppa` (q = 1..15, unscaled data as per the mean-block default), a full Sigma violates it 2/14 times and `refine()` repairs 1, while diagonal and spherical violate it 0/14. Going from `q = 1` to `q = 15` also buys the full Sigma only 514 units of deviance, against 97,056 for the diagonal one: its ~13,000 covariance parameters absorb the structure the clustering would otherwise explain. |
| `covariance_shape_selection_study.R` | Simulation study behind the diagonal default, 12 replicates, both generative regimes, n = 200, true q = 5. At `n/p = 1.3` (p = 150), BIC picks the true `q` with a diagonal Sigma 9/12 (diagonal data) and 10/12 (full data), against 5/12 and 8/12 with a full one: a full Sigma costs the choice of `q`, even when the data *were* generated with it, while the ARI at the true `q` is the same either way. At `n/p = 3.3` (p = 60) the clusters are weaker (ARI about 0.82) and every fit under-selects, the diagonal one most (3/12 on either data); a genuinely full Sigma is then better fitted as such (8/12). |
| `sequential_mean_then_variance.R` | Validates `normal_block_sequential()`. A positive control carrying two genuinely distinct structures recovers both exactly (`q` and ARI = 1.000 on each). On `brca_rppa` the two partitions are unrelated (ARI 0.017; q = 50 in the mean, 19 in the covariance), so a model forcing them equal would be badly misspecified; but the covariance clustering is largely unchanged by removing the mean structure first (ARI 0.715 against fitting it directly), which suggests the two structures are separable rather than competing. |
| `nestor_protocol.R` | Nestor Ngalala Manguitini's M2 simulation protocol (`inst/normalblockmean/code_nestor`), re-run on this package's estimator: his data-generating process verbatim, our estimator, his grid (n in 80/150/220/300, p = 60, q in 3/5, SNR in 0.3/1.5, 10 replicates, lambda = 0.1). 160 configurations, ~37 min (serial). Results in `nestor_protocol_results.rds`, printed output in `nestor_protocol_output.txt`. Four findings: (i) `kmeans` is the best of our four initialisations at both SNRs (mean ARI 0.90 at SNR 0.3, 0.97 at SNR 1.5), with `sbm` a close second and `ward2`/`spectral` clearly behind -- which is what makes `kmeans` this family's default; (ii) RMSE on `BC'` falls with `n` in three of the four (SNR, q) cells, and in the fourth (SNR 0.3, q = 5) only its last step goes up, together with the ARI (0.89 -> 0.84): a clustering failure, not an estimation one; (iii) BIC and ICL select the true `q` in 65-88% of the replicates when q = 3 or SNR = 1.5, but only 38-40% at SNR 0.3 with q = 5, where they under-select (median ICL 4.5), as expected from the per-group penalties of `criteria_selection_study.R` in a weakly separated regime; (iv) RMSE on Sigma does *not* improve with `n`, which is his protocol rather than our estimator -- lambda is fixed at 0.1 while Sigma is rescaled per dataset to hit the target SNR, so the penalty bias dominates. Note his `RMSE(B)` and `RMSE(alpha)` compare quantities identified only up to a permutation of the cluster labels; `RMSE(BC')` is the one that means anything. |
| `nestor_protocol_qsel_unpenalised.R` | Follow-up to the above, separating criterion from estimator. Nestor penalises Omega at lambda = 0.1 but his `calculer_nu()` counts the *dense* p(p+1)/2 regardless, so his BIC is monotone in q by construction while ours -- which counts the non-zero off-diagonal terms, as lasso degrees-of-freedom theory prescribes -- is not. Re-running his grid with a full but **unpenalised** Sigma, where our `nb_param` equals his nu at every q (our penalty still differs: per-group, with log(np) on the cluster profiles and log(p) on the proportions, see `criteria_selection_study.R`), reverses the gap: exact q selection by BIC 82.5/57.5/92.5/85.0% (SNR 0.3 then 1.5, q = 3 then 5) against his 72.5/60.0/90.0/87.5%, with over-selection down from 35/27.5/25/20% to 5/5/5/2.5%. The earlier deficit was the criterion, not the estimator; what remains is under-selection at SNR 0.3, q = 5 (37.5%). Results in `nestor_protocol_qsel_unpenalised.rds`. Caveat: one lambda, one data-generating process. |
| `split_rule_comparison.R` | Default split rule of `refine()` for this family. The same initial collection is refined with `"noise"` (median cut of the noise variances) and `"kmeans"` (2-means on the least-squares trajectories), on `brca_rppa` (5 seeds), `onema` with zero-inflation (3 seeds) and 10 simulated datasets. `"noise"` removes more deviance on every real-data seed (2-15% more on brca, about 8% on onema); the simulations are a tie (7/10 equal, 2 to `"kmeans"`, 1 to `"noise"`); the selected q and the ARI at the true q never differ. `"noise"` stays the default (the variance-block family uses `"kmeans"`, which clearly wins there). |
| `criteria_selection_study.R` | Checks the criteria of PR #55 (per-group penalties after Daudin/Keribin, ICL = BIC + 2 H(tau) after Biernacki) against the previous ones, in both families (despite the folder name). 120 configurations x 50 datasets, simulated from the models; the old and new criteria, and the intermediate variants, are recomputed from the same refined fits. Results in `criteria_selection_study.rds` (selected q per dataset), ~10 min on 20 cores with one BLAS thread each (OMP_NUM_THREADS=OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1). With `scale = FALSE` (well-specified fits), separable regime (ARI >= 0.9 at the true q): **variance-block** BIC old/new 89/89%, ICL old/new 60/89% (dropping the entropy of W from the ICL is what matters; the penalty change is neutral there), and the exact complete-data ICL (W integrated out exactly at the MAP partition) agrees with the variational one (87%); **mean-block** all criteria 91-94%. In the weak regime (ARI < 0.9), every criterion under-selects; the log(np) on the mean-block profiles makes it worse (BIC 24% -> 16% exact), with no over-selection to correct (at most 3% for any criterion). `scale = TRUE` (the default of both families before #60, now of the variance-block family only) makes the models misspecified on these data and every criterion over-selects (mean-block 76-92% of the separable datasets, variance-block 27%). |
| `diagnostic_mean_block_brca.R` | Initialization, convergence and `refine()` on `brca_rppa`. At q = 8, `kmeans` reaches the best ELBO (-39,411, against -40,531 for `ward2` and -40,845 for `spectral`; `best_of_inits` picks it). Over q = 2..70, the cold-started collection violates deviance monotonicity 20 times and `refine()` brings that down to 2, moving the ICL choice from q = 60 to 56. The partition is unrelated to the GO annotation (ARI 0.004) and to the variance-block one (ARI 0.007, q = 28). |
| `diagnostic_mean_block_onema.R` | The same on `onema` (log1p biomass, four standardized environmental covariates, no zero-inflation). `kmeans` again beats `ward2` and `spectral` at q = 8 (`best_of_inits` gains 7 ELBO units more). `refine()` improves 10 of the 14 models of q = 2..15 and moves the ICL choice from q = 12 to 10. |
| `diagnostic_mean_block_scaling.R` | How cost and quality scale with `n`, `p` and `q` (n = 300, p = 150, q = 10, simulated with a full Sigma, fitted diagonal), with a profiling pass. The three initializations end within 6 ELBO units (ARI 0.79-0.81 at the true q); ICL selects q = 7 (ARI 0.72) before and after `refine()`, the under-selection expected when clusters are this weakly separated (see `criteria_selection_study.R`). `refine()` costs 5.5 times the collection fit (4.5 s); the fit itself is entirely spent in C++. |

## Open questions

- **Joint mean + covariance clustering.** The sequential estimator above is a
  heuristic, not a joint model. Free partitions look more plausible than linked
  ones (the two selected `q` differ a lot: 50 and 19 on `brca_rppa`), and the
  separability measured there is an argument for identifiability. What a joint
  fit would buy over two stages -- statistical efficiency, a single criterion --
  is still open, as is the cost of selecting over a `q_mean` x `q_var` grid.
- **When does a full Sigma become viable again?** It wins at a comfortable
  `n/p`; the transition point is not mapped.
